## 1. Strategic Reframing: From Portfolio to Digital Boardroom

### 1.1 The Paradigm Shift

This document supersedes the v1.0 PRD by fundamentally reframing Katherine Newman's digital presence. ## 3. Problem Statement (Refined for Investor Lens)

Katherine's digital presence must solve a fundamentally different problem than a traditional portfolio. She is not demonstrating competence — she is **closing a room**.

### 3.1 What Investors Actually Evaluate

When a pre-seed/seed investor visits a founder's site, they assess four things in under 90 seconds:

1. **Founder-Market Fit** — "Does this person have an unfair advantage in this market?"
2. **Execution Evidence** — "Has this person shipped hard things at scale?"
3. **Vision Clarity** — "Is the opportunity big enough, and does the founder see it clearly?"
4. **Signal Quality** — "Do the external validators confirm what I'm seeing?"

The Digital Boardroom must answer all four within the first viewport and deepen conviction with every scroll.

### 3.2 What Co-founders Actually Evaluate

Technical and product co-founders evaluate differently than investors:

1. **Vision Magnetism** — "Is this a mission I'd dedicate 10 years to?"
2. **Domain Depth** — "Does this founder understand the problem space better than anyone?"
3. **Complementary Gaps** — "Is there a clear role for me that's critical to success?"
4. **Personal Chemistry Signals** — "Do I sense intellectual rigor and emotional intelligence?"

### 3.3 The Digital Boardroom Must:

- Establish **founder-market fit** within 5 seconds (hero section)
- Prove **execution capability** through strategic proof points, not resume lines
- Articulate the **SILOETT thesis** as a market inevitability, not just a good idea
- Create **urgency** — this is a window of opportunity, not an open invitation
- Convert visitors into **high-intent actions** (booking calls, requesting decks)

## 7. Design System: Boardroom Aesthetics

### 7.1 Design Philosophy

The visual language should evoke a **private equity boardroom**: clean, authoritative, understated luxury. Think Blackstone's annual report meets a premium editorial magazine. Every design choice should communicate: "This is a serious person building a serious company."

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**
- No startup cliches (rocket ships, lightbulbs, neon gradients)
- No tech-bro aesthetics (dark mode with green text, Matrix-style)
- No excessive animation (this isn't a creative agency portfolio)
- No stock photography (everything should feel curated and intentional)
- No skill bars, percentage indicators, or gamified elements

### 7.2 Color Palette

```
Primary:      #0F172A  (Deep Navy — Authority, Trust, Executive Presence)
Secondary:    #C8A24E  (Muted Gold — Refined Success, Heritage, not flashy)
Accent:       #3B82F6  (Electric Blue — Forward-thinking, Technology, AI)
Surface:      #F8FAFC  (Off-white — Clean, Premium, Generous Whitespace)
Text:         #1E293B  (Charcoal — Crisp Readability)
Muted:        #64748B  (Slate — Secondary Text, Captions)
```

**Note on Gold:** The secondary color is shifted from bright amber (#F59E0B) to a more muted, sophisticated gold (#C8A24E). Bright amber reads "tech startup." Muted gold reads "established authority." This single change shifts the entire perception from "scrappy founder" to "experienced executive building something new."

### 7.3 Typography

```
Headlines:    "Sora" or "Inter" — Bold, 48-72px (desktop)
```

### 7.4 Visual Principles (Refined for Boardroom Effect)

1. **Generous Whitespace** — The most expensive thing on screen is empty space. Premium sites breathe. Each section should have significant padding, conveying that the content is important enough to stand alone.

2. **Large, Confident Typography** — Headlines should be large and tight-tracked. This communicates authority and clarity of thought. No hedging, no tentative sizing.

3. **Restrained Animation** — Movement should be barely noticeable: subtle scroll-triggered fades, metric counter animations, smooth section transitions. The goal is "refined" not "flashy." Animation should feel like a Bloomberg terminal, not a Dribbble shot.

4. **Editorial Imagery** — If photos of Katherine are available, they should be editorial quality — natural light, candid or semi-posed, environments that suggest leadership (boardroom, Station F, speaking engagements). If no photos are available, use abstract visuals that suggest the convergence of creativity and technology — not literal AI imagery.

5. **Data Visualization** — Metrics should be presented with the visual weight of a financial report. Large numbers, minimal decoration, maximum impact. Think annual report, not infographic.

6. **Subtle Texture** — Very light background textures or grain to avoid a flat, generic feel. The site should feel crafted, not templated.

7. **Dark Mode Option** — For tech-savvy investor audiences who prefer dark interfaces. The dark mode should feel like a premium terminal or Bloomberg screen, not just inverted colors.

## 14. Measuring Success: The Conviction Framework

### Beyond Vanity Metrics

Traditional portfolio metrics (page views, time on site) measure interest. The Digital Boardroom must measure **conviction** — whether visitors move from curiosity to action.

### The Conviction Funnel

```
AWARENESS        →  Visitor lands on the site
                    Metric: Unique visitors, traffic sources

CREDIBILITY      →  Visitor scrolls past the hero
                    Metric: Scroll depth > 25%, time on hero < 10s (they're not confused)

UNDERSTANDING    →  Visitor reads the thesis and track record
                    Metric: Scroll depth > 50%, section time > 30s on thesis

CONVICTION       →  Visitor reaches the invitation section
                    Metric: Scroll depth > 75%, return visits

ACTION           →  Visitor books a call or requests the deck
                    Metric: CTA clicks, calendar bookings, deck downloads

ADVOCACY         →  Visitor shares the site with their network
                    Metric: Referral traffic, LinkedIn mention spikes
```

### Weekly Review Cadence

Katherine should review these metrics weekly for the first month post-launch, then bi-weekly. The site should be treated as a living product, not a static page — continuously optimized based on where visitors drop off in the conviction funnel.
